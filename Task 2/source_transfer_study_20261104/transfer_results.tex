\subsubsection{Frozen transfer
performance}\label{frozen-transfer-performance}

On the 275 eligible external addresses, the frozen tree attains
membership precision 0.966102, recall 0.467213 and F1 0.629834. It
recovers 57 of 122 case sources, misses 65 and assigns two of 153
comparisons to the case class. AP is 0.688142 and ROC-AUC is 0.727151.

\subsubsection{Fixed-rule error
diagnosis}\label{fixed-rule-error-diagnosis}

The root tests float32-converted prior balance against 14,820,511.5
satoshis (about 0.148205 BTC). Its lower-balance branch contained zero
case sources and 642 comparisons in the Coldcard training export. In
CryptoBilis, 63 case sources and 137 comparisons enter this branch. All
63 case sources become false negatives under the unchanged rule. The two
remaining false negatives have higher balances but enter the fixed
inactivity-at-most-1,006.5-block branch. This is an exact replay
explanation, not a newly selected threshold or a causal account of
incident differences.

\subsubsection{Limits and supported
conclusion}\label{limits-and-supported-conclusion}

The operational positive label is linkage to a selected historical
collector payment, not independently verified loss or hardware use.
Comparisons are unattributed outside-reference sources, not certified
legitimate or non-hardware examples. Both collector snapshots belong to
one already inspected incident. Neither the address count nor
measured-link grouping establishes independent victims, devices or
entities; no untouched or prospective test is claimed.

Coverage and quarantine change the evaluation population and class
prevalence. Incomplete prefixes have no vector, while an exhausted prior
prefix does not certify an entire current history. Individual
CryptoBilis full-current-history status remains not collected for this
source. The separate Coldcard comparison full-history counts (864
complete, 136 incomplete) are unchanged. Provider reconciliation,
cryptographic inclusion, source attribution and owner/device evidence
are distinct claims.

The results support a reproducible, limited comparison of source-linkage
profiles. They expose a failure of reliable frozen-tree transport and
show that a prespecified inactivity ranking can outperform that model in
this selected sample. They do not validate theft probabilities,
hardware-wallet identification or population-wide generalization. The
high inactivity AP also does not turn inactivity into a validated theft
detector. Preserving the negative transfer result is scientifically
necessary; adapting the tree or its threshold to these inspected answers
would require a separately specified design and independent evaluation
evidence.
